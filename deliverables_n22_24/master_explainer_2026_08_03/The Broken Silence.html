Overview

Argue one worldview. Then argue its opposite.

The Broken Silence is a two-scene role-playing and on-camera messaging exercise. Players confront a possible exception to Starfleet's Prime Directive after a Federation observation mission accidentally reveals evidence of alien life to a pre-warp civilization.

Each player chooses an archetype for Scene One. After the situation changes, that player adopts the archetype's counterpart in Scene Two. The challenge is to communicate persuasively from one worldview, then reconsider the same crisis through an opposing one.

There is no predetermined correct decision and no conventional winner. The aim is to practise:

  • Embodying a distinct communication style.
  • Making a clear argument under uncertainty.
  • Responding to competing values and perspectives.
  • Speaking persuasively on camera.
  • Recognizing the limitations of one's preferred approach.
Shared rules

The Prime Directive

Every player begins holding the same six rules — and the same ambiguity about what they mean here.

  • Starfleet must not interfere with the natural development of alien civilizations.
  • Formal first contact normally waits until a civilization achieves warp capability.
  • Contact with a pre-warp society risks cultural contamination, political upheaval, and technological dependence.
  • If Federation activity has already contaminated a culture, limited contact may be considered to repair or contain that contamination.
  • Contact does not authorize Starfleet to impose Federation values or provide advanced technology.
  • Even well-intentioned interference can permanently alter another civilization's development.

No interpretation is automatically correct. That uncertainty drives the game.

The reversal

Ten roles. Five players. Each one plays a hinge.

A player chooses a pair, not two independent roles. Whoever takes the Sovereign in Scene One must take the Rebel in Scene Two. No archetype is duplicated; with fewer than five players, use any subset of the pairs.

Briefing

Pelagos IV

The USS Meridian is covertly observing a technologically advanced but pre-warp civilization. Under the Prime Directive, the ship must remain hidden.

The planet

The Pelagans have:

  • Orbital satellites and permanent settlements on two moons.
  • Proof that other habitable planets exist.
  • No confirmed evidence of intelligent alien life.
  • A prototype warp engine most Starfleet scientists believe is years from functioning.
  • Several rival planetary governments with a history of mistrust.

The accident

During an ion storm, a camouflaged Federation observation probe malfunctions and crashes near an isolated Pelagan astronomical observatory. A small group of Pelagan scientists recovers it before Starfleet can. They activate part of its memory system and discover:

  • Star charts showing inhabited Federation worlds.
  • Images of numerous alien species.
  • Evidence that an advanced vessel is presently hiding in their system.
  • The emblem of the United Federation of Planets.

They transmit a narrow-beam message toward the Meridian. Only twelve Pelagan scientists appear to know the truth. Their governments and the general population do not.

Scene One

The Prime Directive Council

Players take five minutes to deliberate on their roles. The captain then calls a confidential meeting: the council must determine whether the Pelagans' discovery justifies contact — or makes continued restraint even more important. It has ten minutes to recommend one of four actions.

On the table

  1. Remain silent and continue observing.
  2. Covertly recover the probe and remove the evidence.
  3. Send a minimal response confirming the message was received.
  4. Initiate controlled first contact, arguing that Federation contamination has already made contact necessary.

The group may invent another option, provided it can be defended under the Prime Directive.

The Sovereign

The Captain

You must make the final decision and accept responsibility for whatever follows. You are responsible both for obeying Starfleet principles and for recognizing when circumstances no longer fit a simple rule. You may listen to everyone, but you cannot hide behind regulations or consensus.

Central question

What decision can you make and personally take responsibility for?

Communication challenge

Create clarity and confidence without pretending that uncertainty has disappeared.

Becomes The Rebel →
The Priest

Keeper of the Directive

You regard the Prime Directive as more than bureaucratic procedure. It expresses humility: advanced civilizations must not reshape others simply because they possess the power to do so. The Pelagans' curiosity does not necessarily give Starfleet the right to enter their history.

Central question

What principle must remain sacred, even when breaking it appears compassionate?

Communication challenge

Defend a principle without using it to avoid the human reality of the situation.

Becomes The Trickster →
The Warrior

The Security Officer

An unknown civilization possesses Federation technology and knows the approximate location of the ship. The scientists may be sincere, but the message could be a trap. If a Pelagan government obtains the probe, Federation technology could alter the planet's military balance.

Central question

What must be protected, and what danger are the others underestimating?

Communication challenge

Create appropriate urgency without allowing fear to make the decision.

Becomes The Healer →
The Judge

Starfleet Legal Officer

Your responsibility is to interpret the Prime Directive consistently. Does accidental exposure constitute contamination? Have twelve scientists discovered alien life, or must their civilization reach that conclusion collectively? Can Starfleet correct its own mistake without making the interference worse?

Central question

What standard should govern this case — and every similar case afterward?

Communication challenge

Make a principled argument while acknowledging facts that do not fit neatly within the rule.

Becomes The Advocate →
The Steward

Cultural Observation Specialist

You have studied Pelagos for years. You think about the civilization it might become if permitted to develop independently. You also know that removing the probe might mean erasing a genuine Pelagan discovery simply because Starfleet dislikes its timing.

Central question

Which action best protects the Pelagans' right to their own future?

Communication challenge

Speak for future generations without overlooking the people making decisions in the present.

Becomes The Visionary →

Scene One performance

  1. Each player gives a recommendation directly to camera — up to 4 minutes each, 20 minutes maximum for the round.
  2. The Sovereign announces the council's final decision.
  3. The group records a short message for the crew explaining why its decision honours the Prime Directive.

The facilitator then reveals the turn.

The Turn

Silence is no longer available.

Before the Meridian can complete its chosen action, two new transmissions arrive.

Pelagan Planetary Defence Authority

Unknown vessel in high orbit: we have confirmed your presence. Identify yourself and explain your activities. If you remain silent, we will treat you as a hostile surveillance platform.

Observatory — private channel

Our government has seized the probe. Some leaders want peaceful contact. Others believe your technology will give them control of our world. They do not speak for everyone. Please do not give them weapons — and please do not disappear.

The Meridian then detects that Pelagos has begun powering its experimental warp engine. The Pelagans request Federation assistance with the test.

Starfleet's original secrecy is no longer possible. The extent and form of contact remain undecided.

Players now flip to their counterpart archetypes. They become a different Starfleet advisory group, brought in to challenge the assumptions of the original council.

Scene Two

The First Contact Assembly

Players take five minutes to deliberate on their new roles. The assembly then has ten minutes to construct the Meridian's first official message to Pelagos.

What must be decided

  • Whether to answer the planetary government.
  • Whether to continue communicating privately with the observatory.
  • Whether to address the entire Pelagan population.
  • How much to reveal about the Federation.
  • Whether to offer any assistance with the warp experiment.
  • Whether the Meridian should remain or leave.
The Rebel

Counterpart of the Sovereign

answers the Captain

You challenge the idea that captains, governments, or institutions automatically possess the right to control first contact. Perhaps the Pelagan government does not speak for its entire world. Perhaps Starfleet's rules are protecting Federation comfort more than Pelagan freedom.

Central question

Whose authority should be resisted, and who has been excluded from the decision?

Communication challenge

Turn dissent into a credible alternative rather than opposition for its own sake.

The Trickster

Counterpart of the Priest

answers the Keeper

Everyone is using grand language: destiny, security, natural development, planetary unity, sacred principles. You expose contradictions. If Starfleet truly believes in non-interference, why was it secretly observing Pelagos? If the Pelagans demand honesty, why are both their scientists and their government sending private messages?

Central question

What solemn assumption falls apart when someone asks the obvious question?

Communication challenge

Use wit and irreverence to reveal truth without making the crisis seem trivial.

Counterpart of the Judge

answers the Legal Officer

The Prime Directive protects civilizations, but civilizations consist of people. You speak for the scientists whose discovery may be suppressed, ordinary Pelagans who know nothing of the crisis, and communities that could be endangered if one government monopolizes Federation contact.

The Visionary

Counterpart of the Steward

answers the Observation Specialist

The Pelagans' future has already changed. You want to imagine a form of first contact that does not require domination, technological dependence, or immediate Federation membership. Contact could begin as a limited exchange between equals rather than a revelation delivered by superior beings.

Central question

What new relationship could exist that neither side has considered?

Communication challenge

Make an unproven possibility feel credible without concealing its risks.

The message must answer five questions

  1. Who are we?
  2. Why were we observing you?
  3. Why did we not introduce ourselves?
  4. What will we offer — or refuse to offer?
  5. What happens next?

Each player gives a statement of up to 4 minutes — 20 minutes maximum for the round — before the group delivers its final message. Every player must also challenge or reconsider at least one argument made by their previous archetype.

Endings

Possible decisions

The game ends with the first official message. Whether Pelagos responds peacefully, fearfully, or with hostility can remain unknown.

  • Make limited contact only with the planetary government.
  • Include the observatory scientists in negotiations.
  • Broadcast a message to the entire planet.
  • Confirm alien life but reveal little about the Federation.
  • Refuse technological assistance while offering dialogue.
  • Assist only with safety procedures for the warp test.
  • Leave Pelagos after explaining the Prime Directive.
  • Remain nearby but delay formal contact until the Pelagans decide how they wish to proceed.
  • Create an entirely different approach consistent with the facts of the story.
Running the game

Facilitator's guide

The facilitator keeps time and may play the ship's computer, a reporter, or an off-camera crew member asking questions.

Preparation

  1. Explain the Prime Directive and the purpose of the exercise.
  2. Read the planet and accident sections aloud.
  3. Let each player choose an archetype pair.
  4. Give players five minutes to deliberate on their Scene One role.
  5. Start the council discussion.

Repeat the five-minute deliberation after the turn is revealed, before the First Contact Assembly begins.

Pressure questions

If the conversation slows, complicate the easy answers — without pushing the group toward a preferred outcome.

  • Does finding a Federation probe make the Pelagans ready for contact?
  • Do twelve scientists have the right to initiate contact for an entire planet?
  • Is covertly recovering the probe a second act of interference?
  • Does the planetary government have the right to control the discovery?
  • Is refusing safety assistance with the warp test non-interference — or negligence?
  • Would sharing only safety information still accelerate Pelagan technology?
  • Is Starfleet protecting Pelagos, or protecting itself from responsibility?
  • What would an honest apology sound like?
  • What is the smallest possible first contact?
  • Can the Federation leave now without making the situation worse?
Debrief

Player reflection

The objective is not to decide who was right. It is to notice how different communication instincts reveal different parts of the same problem.

  1. Which archetype felt more natural?
  2. Which archetype was more difficult to embody?
  3. Which of your two messages was more convincing on camera?
  4. What did your second archetype understand that your first could not?
  5. Did changing archetypes change the decision you personally preferred?
  6. Which speaker created the most trust?
  7. Which speaker made the clearest request of the audience?
  8. Which message would the Pelagans be most likely to believe?
Optional advanced rule

The Echo

In Scene One, each player includes one memorable sentence in their camera statement. In Scene Two, that player must quote, reinterpret, or directly contradict the sentence spoken by their earlier archetype.

The Sovereign“Until we understand the consequences, silence is the only responsible command.”
The Rebel“Silence stopped being responsible when they asked us a direct question.”

This makes the archetype reversal visible, and gives the second scene a stronger connection to the first.

The dramatic question
What does humanity choose to say at the moment silence is no longer possible?
