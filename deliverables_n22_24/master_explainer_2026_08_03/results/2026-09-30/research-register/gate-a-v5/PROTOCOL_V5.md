## 3. Estimand and independence
An elementary observation remains exactly one post-import API operation in a new process that exits afterward. A reported timing replicate is the median of exactly nine such elementary observations of the same case and arm. It is a derived statistic over nine processes, never nine calls in a reused worker. With an odd count this is the fifth order statistic; all nine raw observations are retained. This estimates the typical cold-process call through a fixed median-of-nine sampling procedure, not throughput, startup latency, a nine-call sum, or warm-cache latency. It changes the v4 timing estimand and must not be pooled with v4 timings.

Nine is a prospective fixed robustness choice (median tolerates up to four arbitrarily high members without being determined by them), not calibrated to measured arm speeds. It reduces sensitivity to occasional disruptions; persistent interference and timer quantization can still fail. There is no duration calibration, adaptive batch size, fastest-of selection, subtraction, trimming, or rerun. Process startup, imports, serialization, and common staging remain excluded. Arm-specific environment building, discovery, compilation, extraction, and conversion remain charged inside each single-call interval.
