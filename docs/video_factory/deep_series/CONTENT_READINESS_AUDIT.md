## Partition decisions

### Foundations through CM-IR

Keep Boolean semantics, explicit dense CM, CM-IR identity, and execution paths in separate sequential sections.

This prevents a dense output layout, a shared graph, and an evaluator backend from being narrated as one object.

### Comparator and measurement sequence

Teach AST/CSE/CSE-flat/CM-IR mechanisms and flat lowering before timing boundaries, ratios, and retained results.

Viewers can interpret instruction and timing evidence only after the compared artifacts and boundaries are visible.

### Applications

Keep configuration, circuits, and policy/rule workloads as separate episodes under one task-to-output toolbox map.

Their requested outputs, reuse patterns, revisions, and retained evidence differ enough that one generic application montage would mislead.

### CRSE C milestones

Keep C3-C5 and C6-C8 as deep progression episodes, retain dedicated C9-C11 and C12-C16 episodes, split C17-C20 policy evidence from the task-matched C21-C22 portfolio, and give C23 fresh-source transfer its own evidence episode.

The post-C16 work has two different questions: whether an exact-arm policy transfers and sheds overhead, and whether task-matched methods reveal a worthwhile new representation path. Combining them would hide freshness, lifecycle, and completion-cost boundaries.

### CRSE D and E milestones

Use one chaptered D-D7 evolution episode, keep D8, D9, and D10 separate, and pair E1/E2 only inside distinct BDD and SAT chapters.

D8-D10 have independent promotion decisions; E1/E2 share exact-advice/fallback logic but retain different output contracts and visual systems.

### Provenance

End with a dedicated source-hash and approval-identity episode rather than scattering provenance as footnotes.

Source, claim, content, render, release, content approval, and RunPod authorization form one teachable chain with distinct effects.
