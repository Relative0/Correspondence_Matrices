radius**. The Independent Review's central claims reproduce, the corrected CUDD
merge preserves Audit V3's retractions, and every committed CUDD row has the

The provisional publication headline remains the most honest one:

> On shallow, sparse formulas through nominal n=32, optimized CM is approximately
> at Bitset parity and sometimes modestly faster when the comparison is controlled
> by actual support and output scope. CUDD builds compact symbolic
> representations quickly, but the existing CUDD series is symbolic-build time,

1. nominal `n` is usually not the workload size in the wrapper corpus (median

   directionally conservative but metrically imprecise.

### B2. CUDD integrity — confirmed, with a labeling clarification

The 2,700 wrapper rows all satisfy:

- `cudd_backend=dd.cudd`, `cudd_is_cudd=True`, `status=ok`;
- CM/Bitset complete packed equality;
- selected CUDD correctness flag true;
- exact regeneration of every `(n, trial, live_k)` from seed
  `9_100_000 + 10_000*n + trial`.

Every committed wrapper summary value, percentile, median, ratio, and chart
rounding was reconstructed directly from raw CSV. Both 40-row matched-headline
files also have the claimed identity and correctness, and their summary medians
and ratios reconstruct. The matched-headline run used threshold 7, so it retains
the known live-k≥8 mis-tuning and is not the primary current comparison. The
wrapper campaign used `flat_eval=True`, not words.

For each formula the campaign tries ten seeded random orders. Each build window
times expression-to-BDD construction only; manager declaration, correctness,
order generation, and the other nine searches are outside the reported value.
The backend selects the retained BDD lexicographically by node count and then
build time. However, the public wrapper table/chart uses
`robdd_median_time_s`, the median build time across all ten orders, not the
retained BDD's time or the minimum time. Thus numeric values are valid but the
phrase “best-of-10 build timing” should not be used for those bars.

The recorded correctness mode is 64 sampled assignments for the selected BDD
per formula. The runner also checks every order internally (640 samples total
when all ten builds complete), but only the selected check is returned in the
raw row. It proves deterministic sampled assignment agreement, not packed-output
equality and not exhaustive equivalence.

## C. Three honest comparisons

### C1. Equivalent packed truth-function evaluation — local CM/Bitset audit

This is restricted-scope comparison over exact semantic support, never nominal
ambient `n`. It uses the same serialized formula, exact support, fixed dead
variables, words technology on both sides, complete packed equality, seven
paired/interleaved rounds, and raw observations.

Controlled support results:

| live_k | CM median µs | Bitset median µs | median paired CM/Bitset | p10–p90 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 17.16 | 16.93 | 1.053 | 0.832–1.336 |
| 12 | 25.88 | 27.12 | 0.948 | 0.832–1.145 |
| 16 | 60.43 | 65.60 | 0.925 | 0.735–1.236 |

All 294 executed paired observations were packed-equal. Seven all-live XOR
formulas with `live_k=20..32` were retained as explicit guarded skips; they were
not used in a survivor median. The shallow sparse formulas had `live_k=1..11`.
For those tiny outputs CM's wrapper trailed Bitset (median paired ratios roughly
1.09–1.68 by populated stratum), consistent with wrapper overhead dominating.

CUDD is absent from this table. Existing n=16 matched-headline data shows why:
median CUDD build+assignment extraction is about 0.364 s versus a 75 µs Bitset
run; above n=16 extraction was deliberately not run. Symbolic build time is not
a substitute.

## Interpretation

1. **Fastest CM engine by live_k:** the existing verified crossover remains:
   bigint/flat below six, words at six and above. V4 directly exercised words
   at 8, 12, and 16; it does not claim a newly measured 5/6 crossover curve.
2. **CM versus symmetric Bitset:** CM trails on tiny sparse outputs, is near
   parity at controlled `live_k=8`, and is modestly ahead at controlled 12/16
   in this local run. Dispersion crosses parity, so this is a regime statement,
   not a deterministic per-formula win.
3. **CUDD symbolic construction:** historical data says CUDD excels on compact
   BDDs and is about eight times faster than autoref in that RunPod campaign.
   V4 cannot extend this claim without same-box CUDD.
4. **CUDD packed extraction:** the n=16 matched run is about 0.364 s and thousands
   of times slower than the microsecond CM/Bitset explicit path; larger exact
   extraction is unmeasured/infeasible in the current protocol.
5. **Scaling:** wrapper results are dominated by actual `live_k`, expression
   structure, fixed-binding bookkeeping, and BDD order—not nominal n.
6. **Negative bounds:** CM/Bitset explicit output is guarded above 16 here;
   CUDD cannot enter a packed leaderboard without extraction; BDD build can be
   order-sensitive; shallow sparse formulas do not represent hard BDD families.
